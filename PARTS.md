## Pattern projector: two options

Both options plug into the same Pico channel (GP6), and the software works with either. Print `head.stl` for option A, or `head_led.stl` + `led_cartridge.stl` for option B.

| | **Option A: laser + DOE** | **Option B: LED + printed mask** |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | 650 nm dot laser through a 10k random-dot diffractive element | Red LED backlights a random-dot mask, projected by a 3.6 mm M12 lens |
| Simulated depth accuracy (median) | 0.11–0.18 % (≈0.3–0.45 mm at 250 mm) | 0.23–0.27 % with a film mask (≈0.6 mm), 0.31–0.46 % with a printed mask |
| Depth coverage of the view | 92–98 % | 73–97 % |
| Sourcing | Lasermate DOE is quote-only, single source | All commodity parts, and you print the mask yourself |
| Cost | ~$16 laser + DOE (quote) | ~$30 |

## Notes before ordering

- **Camera:**
  - ELP's own page for the same board family (model ELP-USB3DGS800P-V90, 73° lens) lists the PCB as **80 × 16.5 mm**, with MJPEG 2560×800 at 120 fps. [ELP page](http://www.elpcctv.com/elp-800p-global-shutter-monochrome-synchronous-dual-lens-usb-camera-module-m9-lens-73-degree-p-489.html) (quote only).
  - The lens spacing (baseline) and mounting holes are **not published**. Measure them when the camera arrives so the faceplate can be finalised.
  - Lens options run from 63° to 86°. Choose the ~83–86° "no distortion" version if offered.
- **Camera IR filter vs 650 nm lasers:**
  - Reviews say the ELP stereo camera has an IR-cut filter. Common ones cut off around 650 nm, which is right where the lasers are. Expect the laser lines (and the option-A dots) to come through dimmer, and to vary a little as the lasers warm up.
  - The simulation shows that losing half the light still works fine (0.13 % vs 0.11 % depth error).
  - If lines look weak in `ohs preview`, raise exposure or gain. Other fixes: use 635–638 nm lasers (fewer US sellers), or remove the IR-cut glass from the lens holders.
  - Option B's 625 nm LED is unaffected.
- **Picking laser modules on Amazon.** The listing should show all of these:
  - **"Line"** (or "dot" for option A), not "cross"
  - **650 nm**
  - **5 mW**, not 50 or 58 mW
  - **Ø12 mm** body
  - **3–5 V** supply
  - **adjustable focus**
  
  Packs of 5–10 are cheapest and give you spares. A glass lens is nicer than plastic but not required. Line fan angles of 60–90° are best; 110–120° lines are dimmer.
- **Lasers:**
  - Lengths vary by seller (12×30 to 12×45 mm). Any length works: the bore is open at the back.
  - Make sure the listing says **line** for #2 and **dot** for #3, and **5 mW** (some look-alikes are 58 mW).
  - Ideally choose a 60–90° fan angle; 120° lines are dimmer.
- **DOE (option A):**
  - Lasermate sells by quote: sales@lasermate.com, (909) 718-0999.
  - Ask for a 5 mm PET disc of DOE-RD10K650. The page lists a 6×6 mm active area and ≥2 mm beam, so confirm the disc size.
  - The 30k-dot version (DOE-RD30K650) also works.
- **Trigger button:** the grip now takes a **16 mm** button (Adafruit's), not 12 mm. The STLs are updated.
- **MTS-102:** comes with M6 or 1/4-40 threads; the back-cover hole fits either.
- **Transistors:** Adafruit lists the part as PN2222, without the "A". The pinout is the same E-B-C as the PN2222A, so it works.
